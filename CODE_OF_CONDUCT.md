# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Argue with claims, not with people. Assume the person on the other end is acting
in good faith until they show you otherwise. Disclose your interests.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other project space,
for everyone: contributors, maintainers, and drive-by commenters alike.

## Expected behaviour

- **Critique the work.** "This threshold is unenforceable in a state that
  preempts local noise ordinances" is useful. "You clearly have never read a
  statute" is not.
- **Bring evidence.** A disagreement about facts is settled by sources, or it is
  documented as unsettled.
- **Say where you are coming from.** If you maintain, work for, or are paid by
  a project this change would affect, say so. Disclosed interest disqualifies
  nobody; concealed interest is what erodes trust in a shared dataset.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Personal attacks, harassment, or sustained disruption.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing private information — anyone's, including that of people involved
  in a proposal under discussion.
- Concealing a material interest while advocating for a change.

## Reporting

Report a problem by opening an issue, or privately to the maintainers through
the contact listed on the repository profile if the issue involves a person
rather than a change. Reports are handled discreetly. Retaliation against
someone who reports in good faith is itself a violation.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, revert, or remove contributions and comments that violate
this document, and may block persistent or serious violators. Where the problem
is undisclosed interest, the contribution is reverted regardless of its merit —
the record has to be trustworthy.
